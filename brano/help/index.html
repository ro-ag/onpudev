Getting started

Brano is a score-first music workstation. The music is written as ABC notation, a plain-text format. Brano engraves that text as a page and as DAW-style lanes, plays it with expressive phrasing, and lets you discuss or change it with Maestro.

Install

  • Windows 10 and 11: install from the Microsoft Store.
  • macOS 12 or newer (Apple Silicon): download the DMG from the releases page and drag Brano to Applications.
  • Linux x64: download the .tar.gz from the releases page, unpack it and run brano. It needs glibc 2.35 or newer and ALSA; there is nothing to install.

First five minutes

  1. Open Brano. It starts with a bundled score, Fauré's "Après un rêve", so there is something to play straight away.
  2. Press Space to play. Press it again to pause.
  3. Click the Page and Studio tabs above the score to switch between the engraved page and the lanes.
  4. Open your own music with File ▸ Open…. Brano reads .abc, MusicXML (.xml, .musicxml, .mxl) and its own .brn sessions.
  5. Save with File ▸ Save. A session is one .brn file that holds the score, the mix, your instruments and the Maestro conversations.

No account is needed and nothing is uploaded. Brano has no analytics or telemetry. The only network traffic is what you set up yourself for Maestro.

The window

The score sits in the centre. Around it are three zones you can show or hide: left, lower and right.

AreaWhat it holds
Page (centre)The engraved score on paper, ready to print or export.
Studio (centre)One engraved lane per part on a timeline with a bar ruler and playhead.
Parts (left, in Studio)A header per part: instrument, MIDI channel, mute, solo and freeze.
Properties (left, in Page)Paper size, page breaks, notation size, zoom mode and the page number.
Mixer (lower)A strip per part plus the master: effects, pan, mute, solo, freeze and level.
Perform (lower)How expressively Brano plays the score.
Source (lower)The ABC text editor, diagnostics and history.
Maestro (right)The AI assistant chat.
  • Show or hide a pane with View ▸ Panels, or collapse a whole zone with the three small buttons at the right end of the Seek row.
  • Parts belongs to Studio and Properties belongs to Page, so each one is locked while the other view is active.
  • View ▸ Maestro in Separate Window moves the chat to its own window, which is handy on a second display.
  • View ▸ Reset Layout puts everything back.

On Windows and Linux the menus are in the title bar of the window. On macOS they are in the system menu bar.

Playback

The transport is at the top of the window. From left to right: return to start, play/pause, stop, loop and metronome, followed by the display.

  • Play/pause: Space, unless you are typing in a text field.
  • Stop rewinds to bar 1. Return to start rewinds without changing whether it is playing.
  • Seek: drag the Seek bar, or click anywhere on the Studio ruler or lanes.
  • Tempo: click the Tempo number and type a value from 20 to 300, or drag it up and down. Double-click it to go back to the tempo written in the score.
  • Metronome: click to switch it on. Right-click it to set the click level.
  • Master: drag the fader. Double-click resets it.

The transport tempo only changes what you hear. It never edits the score, and exported notation and MIDI keep the written tempo. The metronome is for monitoring only; it is never part of a freeze or an audio export.

The three dots at the top right show the state of the Source (valid, modified or invalid), the Audio output and Maestro.

Editing the score

ABC is the single source of truth. Every change to the music is a change to the text in the Source pane, whether you type it or Maestro makes it for you.

Edit and apply

  1. Show the pane with View ▸ Panels ▸ Source and stay on the Editor tab, ABC view.
  2. Stop playback. The source cannot be edited while the score is playing.
  3. Type your change. The status chip turns to Modified.
  4. Press Apply, or ⌘ Enter on macOS and Ctrl Enter on Windows and Linux. Brano compiles the text and redraws the score.

Edits are not compiled as you type, so a half-written bar never breaks the score you are looking at.

When something is wrong

If the text does not compile, the chip shows Invalid and the Diagnostics tab lists each problem. Click a row to jump to that line in the editor. The score keeps showing the last version that compiled.

History

The History tab lists earlier revisions, recorded each time the session is saved. Restore replaces the source with that revision; the text you leave stays in the history, so restoring is never destructive. Right-click a row to copy its text.

New to ABC?

A tune starts with a few header lines (T: title, M: metre, L: default note length, K: key), followed by the notes. C D E F are notes, c d e f the octave above, z a rest, | a bar line, and a number after a note multiplies its length. File ▸ New gives you a minimal tune to start from, and the ABC notation site has tutorials. Maestro can also write or explain ABC for you.

Files and sessions

  • File ▸ Open… accepts .brn, .abc, .xml, .musicxml and .mxl. There is no separate import command: opening an ABC or MusicXML file imports it. MusicXML is converted to editable ABC where possible. The original file is never modified.
  • File ▸ Save and Save As… write a .brn session: the source and its history, instruments, effects, mixer, Perform settings, tempo override and Maestro conversations.
  • File ▸ Open Recent keeps the last ten files.
  • There is no autosave. A dot next to the logo marks unsaved changes, and Brano asks before closing a session that has them.

Instruments and effects

Every part starts on a built-in General MIDI sound, so a new score plays without any setup. You can replace any part with a plug-in instrument.

Choose an instrument

  1. Open View ▸ Instruments…, or click the sound-source button on a part's header in Studio.
  2. Open the menu for the part, search, and pick a General MIDI program or a plug-in.
  3. Use Open native editor to open the plug-in's own window and choose a sound there.

Brano hosts VST3 instruments on every platform and Audio Units (AUv2) on macOS. The first time, run Scan plug-ins in Settings ▸ Plug-ins. The same page lets you hide plug-ins from the pickers and, on macOS, choose the preferred format when a product is installed as both.

Each plug-in runs in its own process. If one crashes, the part shows a fault with a retry button and the rest of the session keeps running.

Effects

In the Mixer, click + FX on a part or on the master to manage its effect chain: add, reorder, bypass, remove, or open an effect's editor. Part effects come before the part's volume and pan. Master effects come after the master volume.

Freeze

The snowflake button on a part renders it to audio once and plays that render, which saves CPU with heavy instruments. Click it again to return to the live instrument. Freeze All on the master strip does every eligible part.

Banks

A bank lets one multitimbral plug-in (Kontakt, EastWest Opus, HALion, ARIA Player) play several parts, each on its own MIDI channel. Set it up in View ▸ Banks… with Group into bank…. Brano cannot see inside the plug-in, so set each instrument's MIDI channel there to match the channel table in the dialog.

Audio output

Settings ▸ Audio chooses the output device, sample rate, channels and buffer size. If the status dot says No Audio, the saved device is probably disconnected: pick Automatic (system default) and apply.

Perform

Brano does not play the notes mechanically. The Perform tab decides how much interpretation is added on top of what is written.

  • Style: Default, Medieval, Renaissance, Baroque, Classical, Romantic, Pop / Rock, Jazz / Swing and AI Pianist. AI Pianist needs an optional add-on from Settings ▸ Add-ons; until it is installed it plays like Default.
  • Feel: Expression goes from 0% (the notes exactly as written) to 100% (the full style). Swing amount sets how far off-beats are delayed. Style preset puts both back to 100%.
  • Interpretation: switch Dynamics, Accents, Articulation, Pedal and Jitter on or off.
  • Timing & phrasing: Ritardando, Phrase arch, Micro-timing and Swing feel.

Hover over any control for a one-line description. These settings are saved in the session, and MIDI export can use them (Humanized) or ignore them (Flat).

Maestro

Maestro is the AI assistant in the right-hand pane. It reads the notation itself, so it can explain the harmony, check voice leading, review the score before you export, and make changes to the ABC when you ask.

Maestro is off until you connect it to an AI provider. Brano does not include a model and does not sell AI usage: you bring your own API key, or run a model on your own computer.

Set it up

  1. Open Settings… (⌘ , or Ctrl ,) and choose Maestro. Clicking the provider badge at the top of the Maestro pane goes to the same place.
  2. Under Backend choose OpenAI-compatible, or Anthropic if you have a Claude API key.
  3. Pick a Provider. The address and a recommended model are filled in. See low-cost AI providers for which one to choose.
  4. Click Get an API key →. It opens the provider's page where you create a key. Copy the key and paste it into API key.
  5. Press Test to send a short request, then Apply.

The status line in the Maestro pane should now read "Listening to your score". If it shows "Check Maestro configuration", press Details to read the provider's message and Configure to go back to the settings.

Ask

Type in Ask Maestro… and press Enter (Shift Enter for a new line). Some things to try:

  • "Explain the harmony of bars 1 to 8."
  • "Check the voice leading between the voice and the piano left hand."
  • "Review the score before I export it."
  • "Transpose the whole piece down a major second."
  • "Add a four-bar introduction for the piano."

Maestro replies in the language of the interface unless you choose otherwise in Reply language. Lyrics, titles and ABC are never translated.

When Maestro changes the score

Maestro edits the ABC through checked tools: each change must compile before it is accepted. The reply shows a diff of what changed with two buttons: Keep and Roll back. Every earlier version is also in Source ▸ History. Maestro changes instruments or proposes bank wiring only when you ask, and wiring changes wait for your approval.

Options worth knowing

  • Web search and Image input are off by default and only available with providers that support them. With image input on, paste or drop a PNG or JPEG into the prompt, for example a photo of a printed score.
  • Session budget: tick Limit spend to stop sending requests once a session has cost a set amount.
  • Refresh models asks the provider for its current model list. Custom model id… lets you type a model that is not listed.
  • Chats in the Maestro header starts a new conversation or returns to an earlier one. Conversations are saved in the session file.

Privacy and your key

  • When you send a message, the score's ABC and your conversation go to the provider you chose, and nowhere else. Nothing is sent while Maestro is idle or not configured.
  • To keep everything on your computer, use the Ollama (local, keyless) provider.
  • Your API key is stored on your computer in Brano's settings folder, in the file maestro.json, as plain text. Treat that file like a password: do not share it, and if a key leaks, revoke it on the provider's site.

Low-cost AI providers

Maestro works with any of the providers below. A typical question about a score costs a fraction of a cent with the inexpensive models, and several providers have a free tier that is enough to try Maestro.

Maestro needs a model that supports tool calling and streaming. A large context window helps with long scores. The models suggested by each preset meet those requirements.

Prices are in US dollars per million tokens (input / output) as published in November 2026. They change often, and free tiers come with rate limits. Always check the provider's own pricing page, linked in the table. Brano has no commercial relationship with any of these providers.

Keeping the bill small

  • Tick Limit spend under Session budget. Requests stop when the session reaches the amount.
  • Start a New chat for a new topic. A long conversation is sent again with every message.
  • Use a Flash model for everyday questions and switch to a flagship model for a full review.
  • Where a Thinking switch is shown, turning it off makes replies faster and cheaper.
  • Leave Web search off unless you need it; providers charge for each search.

Export

Open File ▸ Export… (⌘ E or Ctrl E), choose a format, press Export… and pick where to save.

FormatWhat you get
PDFOne multi-page PDF of the engraved score.
SVGOne SVG per page. Multi-page scores are saved as a .zip.
PNGPage images at 1×, 2× or 3×. Multi-page scores are saved as a .zip.
TIFFThe current page, or all pages in one multi-page TIFF, at 1×, 2× or 3×.
MusicXMLThe compiled score, for other notation programs.
ABCThe current source text.
MIDIHumanized (with the current Perform settings) or Flat (exactly as written).
WAV, FLACAn audio bounce at 44.1 kHz, 16-bit stereo: one mixed file, or stems with one file per part.

Audio follows your instruments, effects and mixer. Stems keep each part's level and pan, ignore mute and solo, and leave out the master effects. Paper size, page breaks and notation size for the page formats are set in Properties in the Page view.

In the Studio view

  • Click the ruler or a lane to move the playhead.
  • ⌘ + wheel, or pinch on a trackpad, zooms, keeping your place in the music.
  • Wheel scrolls vertically; Shift + wheel scrolls horizontally. Two fingers pan on a trackpad.
  • Right-click the score for zoom, actual size and Fit height.
  • Right-click a part for mute, solo and freeze.

In the Page view

  • Scroll or drag to move around the page.
  • Zoom with the View menu, or set Fit width or Fit page in Properties.

Settings

Settings… has five pages: General, Audio, Plug-ins, Maestro and Add-ons.

  • Theme: Studio Dark, Graphite and Midnight are dark; Yelcho and Platinum are light. Also in View ▸ Theme.
  • Language: English or Español. Also in View ▸ Language.
  • Reduce motion stops pulses and fades.
  • Show engine diagnostics adds technical details to the status bar, useful when reporting a problem.

Where Brano keeps its settings

SystemFolder
macOS~/Library/Application Support/brano
Windows%APPDATA%\brano
Linux~/.config/brano

The folder holds small JSON files such as ui.json (theme and language), layout.json (panes), audio.json and maestro.json (provider, model and API key). Your music is not stored there: it lives in the .brn files you save.

Troubleshooting

There is no sound

  • Look at the Audio dot at the top right. If it says No Audio, open Settings ▸ Audio, choose Automatic (system default) or a connected device, and apply.
  • Check the Master fader, and that no part is muted while another is soloed.
  • A plug-in instrument can load without a sound selected. Open its editor and load one. Brano reports a silent part rather than replacing it with a General MIDI sound.

A plug-in is missing or keeps failing

  • Run Rescan in Settings ▸ Plug-ins. Plug-ins that failed the scan are listed under "Skipped or incompatible" and can be retried.
  • A faulted part or effect shows a retry button. The rest of the session is not affected.

Maestro does not answer

  • "Check Maestro configuration": no provider is set up yet, or the key was rejected. Press Configure and follow Set it up.
  • "Reply failed": press Details for the provider's message. The usual causes are an expired key, no credit left, a free-tier rate limit, or a model name the provider has retired. Refresh models fetches the current list.
  • With Ollama, make sure the Ollama app is running and that you have pulled the model named in the settings.

My edit will not apply

  • Stop playback first, and make sure the Source pane is on the ABC view. The XML view is read-only.
  • If the chip says Invalid, open Diagnostics and fix the listed lines.

Still stuck?

Write to support@onpu.dev with your operating system, the Brano version from the About window, and what you were doing. Release notes are on the releases page.
